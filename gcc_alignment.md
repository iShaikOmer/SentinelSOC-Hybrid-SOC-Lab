# GCC relevance — control-intent crosswalk (not a compliance claim)

This portfolio lab targets cybersecurity analyst jobs in banking, telecom, managed security services and other regulated organizations in the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Qatar.

| Lab practice | Security-control objective | Example related frameworks |
|---|---|---|
| Centralized authentication and Windows telemetry | Security event logging and monitoring | NIST CSF, CIS Controls, ISO/IEC 27001 |
| Identity spray and privileged group monitoring | Identity and access risk detection | Zero Trust, NIST identity/security guidance, CIS |
| Documented investigation and approval-gated containment | Incident management and response | ISO/IEC 27001, NIST CSF |
| Least privilege for Logic Apps/service identities | Access control and privilege governance | NIST, CIS, ISO/IEC 27001 |
| Retained alert evidence and documented changes | Auditable operations and change control | ISO/IEC 27001, internal SOC procedures |

**Region-specific positioning:** Familiarity with the terms **Saudi NCA ECC** and **UAE Information Assurance / NESA** may help in interviews, but this project is **not mapped to exact NCA ECC or UAE IA control IDs** and cannot establish compliance with either. A formal mapping requires the authorized current framework text, legal applicability, a defined scope and audit evidence. Do not assert that this lab satisfies NCA ECC, NESA, Qatar regulation, ISO 27001, or SOC 2.

**Interview talking point:** “The project demonstrates transferable monitoring, logging, access control and incident-response practices that are relevant to regulated GCC environments. Formal control mapping and compliance testing would depend on the employer's scope and policies.”
